Diarrhoeal Disease

Diarrhoeal disease ranked as the third leading cause of death among under 5 years old.

01. What is Diarrhoea?

Diarrhoea is defined as the passage of three or more loose or liquid stools per day.

It constitutes as a significant global health concern, particularly for children under 5 years old.

It ranks as the second leading cause of mortality and morbidity in this age group worldwide and stands as the primary cause of malnutrition in sub-Saharan Africa.

02. Causes

  1. 1. Inadequate hygiene
  2. 2. Limited access to clean water
  3. 3. Socioeconomic disparities

03. Symptoms

Frequent bowel movements: passing stools more than usual, which may be loose or watery.

Stomachache: discomfort or pain in the abdominal region.

Dehydration: dry mouth, increased thirst, weakness, fatigue, dizziness, and decreased urine output.

Nausea and vomiting: discomfort in the stomach which may be cause to vomit.

Health Reminders – Great Daily Habits

Why are daily habits so important?

Simple daily hygiene habits helps you from getting sick and spreading germs.

The organisms causing diarrhea are transmitted from person to person through food and water contaminated with feces, or through person-to-person contact.

1) Hand Washing

Hand washing is a habit that can help people stay healthier, because it reduces their exposure to microorganisms that can cause diseases.

Hand washing after defecation, or after cleaning a baby's bottom, and before preparing and eating food, can also reduce the risk of diarrhea.

2) Food Preparing

Proper food handling and preparation, including washing fruits and vegetables and cooking food thoroughly, helps eliminate pathogens that can contaminate food and lead to diarrhea.

3) Sanitation

Maintain clean living spaces and using proper sanitation facilities (toilets, etc.) reduces the risk of fecal contamination of food and water sources.

4) Safe Water

Diarrhea is more common in areas lacking proper sanitation, hygiene, and access to safe water.

Clean water usage and improved sanitation facilities are crucial, as inadequate sanitation can quadruple the likelihood of acute diarrhea in children.
